**Established:** Unknown  
**Organization Type:** Private Military Organization
**Origin:** Unknown 

**Unfair Affairs**, commonly abbreviated as **UA**, is a primarily private military organization led by [[Major Althir Vaelis Daelthorn|Althir Vaelis]]. The organization draws personnel from across the world, with a notable tendency to recruit highly capable individuals who share Christian ideology. Unlike many comparable organizations, UA is known to have participated extensively in conventional warfare, contributing forces to numerous conflicts rather than limiting itself primarily to individual operations or specialized missions.

No nation is currently known to exert significant influence over Unfair Affairs. Despite its international composition and extensive involvement in armed conflicts, remarkably little reliable information concerning the organization's internal structure, resources, or activities is available to outsiders.

UA operates as a relatively fluid organization composed primarily of independent members. Although a central structure exists around Vaelis and his authority as the organization's leader, its members are not bound by formal oaths and are subject to comparatively few institutional restrictions. The principal obligation shared between members is a code of honor that prohibits betrayal and preserves the organization's internal cohesion.

This independence extends to the acceptance of orders. Members retain the right to reject commands or decline summons at their discretion, with no formal mechanism compelling their participation in a given operation. This freedom is balanced by the organization's control over its resources. Members who consistently refuse orders or remain uninvolved may find themselves receiving less access to supplies, equipment, and other organizational support than those who cooperate with UA's leadership.

Unfair Affairs therefore maintains an unusual balance between centralized authority and individual autonomy. Its members are free to determine their own participation, while the organization retains sufficient control over its resources to reward cooperation and discourage persistent refusal. The result is a military organization that functions less as a conventional hierarchy and more as a network of independent personnel bound together by loyalty, mutual interest, and a shared code of conduct.
