**Estimated Period: 1 B.C.–1 A.C.**
**Type:** Historical Anomaly

The Null is the name given to a poorly understood period believed to have occurred between 1 B.C. and 1 A.C. Historical records immediately preceding and following this era remain sufficiently intact for scholars to establish that a significant discontinuity exists between them. Beyond this point, however, no direct historical records concerning the period itself have survived.

The absence of information from this era is the origin of its name. Despite extensive study, no surviving documents have been conclusively dated to The Null, and no reliable contemporary account has been recovered. Historians have nevertheless attempted to reconstruct portions of the period through records created before and after it, drawing connections between surviving accounts and the changes observed in the centuries that followed.

The duration of The Null remains unknown. The dates traditionally assigned to its beginning and end are themselves provisional, and there is no evidence establishing that the period lasted precisely two years. Many scholars consider this estimate insufficient, with some proposing that The Null persisted for at least thirty years. This theory is largely based upon the number and scale of historical developments that appear to have occurred during the gap.

Among the many unresolved questions surrounding The Null is the identity and historical existence of the figure now widely known as Jesus Christ. Numerous surviving traditions place his life within this period, although no direct evidence has been recovered that can conclusively confirm either his existence or the circumstances traditionally attributed to him. Interpretations of these traditions vary considerably between academic and religious scholarship.

Despite disagreements regarding the events, individuals, and causes associated with The Null, there is broad scholarly agreement that the period was of extraordinary consequence. The civilizations that emerged from it possessed technologies, phenomena, and concepts that have no known precedents in the surviving records of earlier history.

The most notable example is the phenomenon now known as **splicing**. Artifacts demonstrating the effects or principles of splicing are commonplace in the modern age, yet no confirmed example predating The Null has been identified. Its apparent emergence during the missing period has led many historians to regard the phenomenon as one of the strongest indications that events of profound significance occurred within The Null.

Until direct evidence is recovered, however, the true nature of the period—and the events responsible for the changes that followed—remains unknown.
