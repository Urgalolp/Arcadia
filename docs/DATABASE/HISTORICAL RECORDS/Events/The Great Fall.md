**Date:** Unknown; Pre-Null
**Type:** Cataclysmic Historical Event

The **Great Fall** refers to the destruction of the World Tree, an ancient entity believed to have existed since before recorded history. Though accounts of its appearance, nature, and purpose vary considerably between surviving civilizations, nearly all traditions agree upon one fact: the World Tree was real, and its fall marked the end of an age.

The World Tree was said to have stood at the center of the world, its roots extending into realms beyond the material and its branches reaching toward the heavens. It was believed to serve as a bridge between worlds, connecting the material plane to the various realms beyond it. Some traditions describe it as a source of life itself, while others claim it merely served as a conduit through which magic, souls, and divine influence could pass.

Very little is known regarding the circumstances that led to its destruction. Surviving records contradict one another, with some describing a great war, others a deliberate act of divine intervention, and others still claiming that the Tree simply reached the end of its natural existence. A particularly persistent account attributes its destruction to an unnamed individual who sought to sever the world's connection to the planes beyond it.

Regardless of the cause, the consequences were extraordinary.

When the World Tree fell, its branches reportedly tore through the boundaries between realms, while its roots were ripped from the earth with enough force to permanently alter the surrounding land. Entire forests are said to have been destroyed in moments, while regions distant from the Tree experienced earthquakes, storms, magical disturbances, and other unexplained phenomena.

The fall also appears to have fundamentally altered the relationship between the material world and the planes beyond it. Connections that had previously been stable became unreliable or disappeared entirely. Certain forms of magic ceased to function as they once had, while others became considerably more difficult to perform. Some scholars consequently consider the Great Fall to be the beginning of the gradual decline in planar influence that eventually became characteristic of the modern world.

The remnants of the World Tree are believed to have survived in some form. Ancient wood, seeds, roots, and other unidentified fragments have periodically been discovered throughout history, although their authenticity is often disputed. Several artifacts of immense power have likewise been attributed to the Tree, though no surviving specimen has been conclusively proven to originate from it.

The exact date of the Great Fall cannot be established. The event predates the **Null** by an unknown amount of time, and records from the period immediately surrounding it are either absent or so fragmented that they cannot be reliably reconstructed. Indeed, several civilizations appear to have possessed completely different calendars before and after the event.

For this reason, the Great Fall is regarded not merely as the destruction of an ancient landmark, but as one of the principal dividing points in recorded history. Whatever the World Tree truly was, its destruction permanently changed the world that followed.

The age before it is commonly referred to by scholars as **the Age of the Tree**.
