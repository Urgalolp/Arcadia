**Date:** 621–724
**Type:** World War

The Incursion was a prolonged military advance undertaken by the three Grand Liches Azrakul, Zorakheth, and Ozymor across the region now known as Europe. Beginning in 621, the campaign covered a vast expanse of territory and was accompanied by an army composed primarily of undead creatures and contracted devils. Under the command and influence of the three liches, the army devastated settlements and territories across the continent, resulting in the deaths of countless innocents. The remains of those slain were subsequently raised and incorporated into the advancing forces, allowing the army to continually replenish its numbers.

The precise objectives of Azrakul, Zorakheth, and Ozymor remain uncertain. Contemporary records are scarce, and surviving accounts frequently contradict one another. Historians generally attribute several possible objectives to the Incursion, including the conquest of the known world, the construction of a country-sized necropolis capable of resisting divine influence, and the complete eradication of dragons without regard for alignment or allegiance.

Of these proposed objectives, the extermination of dragons is the most widely accepted. This conclusion is supported by the well-documented hatred of dragons held by Azrakul and Ozymor, as well as by the considerable decline in dragon populations that followed the Incursion. While the relationship between the campaign and the present scarcity of dragons cannot be established in every case, most scholars consider the devastation inflicted upon dragonkind during this period to be the principal cause of its decline.

A significant portion of the magic employed by the forces of the Incursion appears to have originated from a corruption of fae practices. Surviving records describe an unnatural fusion of a hag's covenant with the forces of undeath, accomplished through forced fusion and the fracturing of souls. The precise method by which such magic was developed remains unknown. Several historical accounts suggest the possibility of divine interference, though no surviving evidence has conclusively established this theory.

The consequences of the Incursion extended far beyond the territories directly occupied by its armies. Numerous heroic lineages were extinguished during the conflict, while several pantheons suffered losses among their followers on a scale sufficient to diminish their divine power. Countless artifacts of antiquity were destroyed or lost, and with them disappeared much of the knowledge surrounding ancient myths, legends, and civilizations. In several regions, the destruction wrought during the Incursion permanently altered the historical record, leaving later scholars to reconstruct events from incomplete and fragmented sources.

The Incursion continued for eighty-three years before its conclusion in 724. Its decisive turning point occurred in 700, when Zorakheth betrayed the covenant shared by the three Grand Liches. The betrayal severely weakened the remaining forces of Azrakul and Ozymor, but did not immediately bring the conflict to an end. Their eventual destruction was achieved only through a prolonged war of attrition, during which countless members of numerous races, armies, and heroic orders were killed.

Many historians maintain that Zorakheth's betrayal was the only event that prevented the Incursion from achieving its objectives. According to this interpretation, had the covenant remained intact, the combined forces of the three Grand Liches would have encountered little opposition capable of halting their advance. Whether this conclusion reflects historical reality or merely the scale of devastation observed during the war remains a subject of scholarly debate.

Today, the Incursion is regarded as one of the most destructive periods in recorded history. Its effects remain evident in the diminished population of dragons, the disappearance of ancient knowledge, the extinction of numerous lineages, and the lasting scars left upon the cultures and territories that survived it.
