Electrical tools allow you to work with electrical and electronic systems, including wiring, power systems, circuitry, and electrically powered devices. They are used to install, maintain, repair, test, and modify electrical equipment and to diagnose faults that cannot be identified through ordinary inspection.

**Components.** Electrical tools include insulated screwdrivers, wire cutters and strippers, pliers, a multimeter, electrical tape, soldering equipment, wire, connectors, and assorted small electronic components.

**Investigation.** Your expertise allows you to diagnose electrical faults, identify damaged components, trace circuits, and recognize signs of tampering or sabotage.

**Perception.** You can identify subtle electrical problems through visual inspection or by recognizing unusual sounds, smells, heat, sparks, or other signs of malfunction.

**Repair.** With access to your tools and suitable replacement components, you can restore damaged electrical equipment. The time and materials required depend on the complexity of the device and the nature of the damage.

**Technology.** Your familiarity with electrical systems gives you additional insight when examining electronic or electrically powered devices, particularly when determining how a device receives, stores, or distributes power.

Some activities can be done with more ease when using this tool, and some can only be done with this tool, such as the exceptions (and their DCs) below.

- Repair damaged wiring (10)
- Diagnose an electrical fault (10)
- Replace a damaged electrical component (10)
- Disable an electrical device without damaging it (15)
- Bypass a basic electrical circuit (15)
- Repair a damaged electronic device (15)
- Modify an electrical device for a new purpose (20)
- Reconstruct a damaged circuit from its components (20)
